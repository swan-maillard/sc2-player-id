    What you're looking at

    StarCraft II is a real-time strategy game. Good players lean on hotkeys: they bind groups of units or buildings to the keys 0 to 9 and jump between them dozens of times a minute. The competition gave us 3,052 games from 200 players, each one a single line of tokens: s for a selection, Base for the main building, hotkey32 for "slot 3, used", and markers like t5, t10, t15 that keep track of game time in seconds.

    A classifier needs the same columns for every game, and these lines run from a handful of tokens to over 10,000. So we cut each game at 25 minutes, split it into the windows between markers, file each window under start (first minute), early (up to 6 minutes) or mid (up to 25), and count per second: presses of each of the 10 hotkey slots, hotkeys created and used, and selections of the base, of minerals and of anything else. That's 15 rates for 3 phases, plus the race as three 0 or 1 columns.

    The real games aren't public: the data sat on Kaggle behind the class competition. What you can load above are the only bits of real logs our notebooks printed, the first seconds of ten games and the competition's own example. That's enough to see the habit, though: training games 0, 2 and 4 are all player 53, and all three create hotkey 3 within seconds.

    The importance chart is the piece I worked on. The two measures rank things differently, but both put the first minute's hotkeys near the top: how a player sets up their keys right after the game starts is close to a signature.
